For real estate agents

Open house follow-up texts and emails: templates by visitor type

By Plain English Library · Updated September 26, 2026

Most open house visitors hear nothing afterward, or they get the same "thanks for stopping by" as everyone else. The agents who turn sign-ins into clients send something short, personal and fast, and they send a different message to each kind of visitor.

Three rules that do most of the work

  • Fast. HousingWire's open house follow-up guide recommends sending within 24 hours, while the house and you are still fresh in their mind. A same-evening text is better still.
  • Specific. Mention one thing they said or reacted to. It proves the message is not a mass email.
  • One easy next step. A yes-or-no question beats "let me know if you have any questions."

Make this easy on yourself: at sign-in, add a small notes column and write one word per visitor ("kitchen," "schools question," "neighbor," "has agent"). That word is your personalization.

Templates by visitor type

1. Active buyer without an agent

Text, same evening: "Hi [Name], it's [You] from the open house on [Street]. You mentioned [the kitchen / needing a home office]. Want me to send two or three similar homes that just came up?"

Email, next morning. Subject: "Great meeting you at [Street]"

"Hi [Name], thanks for coming by [Street] yesterday. Here are the details you asked about: [one fact, such as the HOA fee or the roof age, confirmed]. Based on what you said, [one or two similar homes with links]. Would a quick 15-minute call this week help you figure out what to focus on?"

2. Just looking, or early in the process

Email, next day. Subject: "The [Street] details, and no pressure"

"Hi [Name], thanks for stopping by. If you're just starting to look, the most useful thing I can send is a short list of what homes like this are actually selling for nearby. Want me to set that up? One email a month, easy to stop."

3. Neighbor

Neighbors are often future sellers, and they came for a reason.

Email or handwritten note, within two days: "Hi [Name], great to meet a neighbor at [Street]. If you ever wonder what your own home might sell for, I'm happy to put together a no-obligation estimate. And if you know anyone who'd love to live on your street, I'd be grateful for the introduction."

4. Buyer who already has an agent

Respect the relationship. Do not solicit them as a client.

Short email, next day: "Hi [Name], thanks for visiting [Street]. If you or your agent need the disclosures or have questions about the home, I'm happy to send anything over to them directly."

A follow-up schedule that isn't pushy

WhenWhat
Same eveningShort personal text (active buyers who gave permission to text)
Next morningEmail with the one fact they asked about, plus a next step

The AI prompt that personalizes every message

[Paste your Agent Profile or three examples of your own writing]

I hosted an open house at [address, or "a 3-bed home in [area]"].
Visitor: [first name only]. Type: [active buyer / just looking / neighbor / has an agent].
My note from sign-in: "[their one word or what they said]".
Confirmed facts I can share: [only facts you have checked].

Write (1) a same-evening text under 40 words and (2) a next-morning email under 100 words with a subject line.
Mention my note naturally. End with one yes-or-no question. Do not invent any facts about the home or the area. Do not describe the neighborhood in terms of who lives there. Use [CONFIRM] for anything I need to check.

Use first names only. As covered in our privacy checklist, phone numbers, emails and anything a visitor told you in confidence should stay out of general AI tools.

Compliance in two minutes

  • Fair Housing covers what you say, not just listings. The HUD rule at 24 CFR 100.75 applies to written and oral statements. Talk about the home and verifiable facts, never about who a neighborhood is "right for." If asked about schools or safety, point people to official sources rather than giving your opinion.
  • Email: once a visitor moves onto your regular marketing emails, those emails need your postal address and a working opt-out under the FTC's CAN-SPAM rules.
  • Texting: add "OK to text me" as a checkbox on the sign-in sheet and send personal, one-to-one texts. If you use automated or bulk texting software, ask your broker how your setup meets the telemarketing rules the FCC enforces under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act.
