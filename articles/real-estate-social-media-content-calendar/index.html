For real estate agents

A real estate social media content calendar you can use for the next 30 days

By Plain English Library · Published October 3, 2026

Use this real estate social media content calendar to plan 12 posts over 30 days. You get a four-week schedule, the facts to gather for each post, and AI prompts for turning your notes into captions. Start with three posts a week on one platform, then adjust the pace to what you can maintain.

You do not need a new listing for each post. A question from a buyer consultation, an explanation of your showing process, or a lesson from preparing a home for photos can give readers a reason to contact you.

Choose one reader and one next step

Before writing, decide who you want to help this month. Try “buyers planning their first consultation in my market” or “homeowners preparing to list.” Write about the stage of the transaction and the questions people ask there. Avoid guessing a reader's finances, household, or preferences from their age or appearance.

Choose one useful action for the reader: save a checklist, send a question, or book a conversation. Asking for a call under every post can make a helpful account feel like a sales script. Match the invitation to the topic.

Create a working document with these columns: planned date, topic, source notes, format, caption, image permission, review status, and result. A spreadsheet or a plain table works. Keep the source notes next to the draft so you can check a number without searching through old messages.

This is a suggested starting schedule, not a claim about the best posting frequency. Choose any three publishing days in each of the first four weeks. Use the last two days to review results and prepare next month's topics.

AI prompts for real estate social media posts

Give the AI your material before asking for polished copy. Replace the brackets below with information you can check. Keep client identities, private financial details, and unpublished transaction information out of the prompt.

Prompt 1: Turn your notes into a weekly plan

Help me plan three real estate social posts for next week.

Reader and transaction stage: [describe].
Platform: [choose one].
My tone: [describe in a sentence].
My source notes: [paste public, verified notes with source dates].
My available visuals: [list images or video I own or may use].
My brokerage's required wording: [paste approved wording].

Return three rows: topic, reader question, format, facts needed, and one useful next step.
Use only the supplied facts. Mark missing information [CHECK].
Do not invent listings, client stories, testimonials, market statistics, or local events.
Keep each post focused on a different question.

Prompt 2: Draft one caption from the approved plan

Draft the caption for [selected post] using the notes below.
Length: 80 to 120 words.
Start with the reader's specific question or problem.
Give one useful explanation and end with the chosen next step.
Use plain words and my tone. Avoid sales pressure and predictions.

After the caption, list each factual claim beside the source note supporting it.
If a claim has no support, remove it or mark it [CHECK].
Keep my approved brokerage wording intact.
Notes: [paste].
Next step: [save / ask a question / visit a resource / book a conversation].

An AI's claim check is a drafting aid. Read the source yourself before posting. A model can label a sentence supported even when it has misunderstood the number or its context.

Prompt 3: Adapt a checked caption into a short video

Turn this approved caption into a 30-second speaking script.
Use a question, a plain explanation, and one next step.
Suggest three simple shots I can record myself.
Do not add claims or property details.
Separate the spoken script from the shot list.
Caption: [paste checked caption].

A worked example without a listing

Suppose your source notes say: “At consultations I ask buyers to separate essential features from preferences. A quiet workspace may mean a spare bedroom, a den, or part of another room. I ask which arrangement would work.” This is a process example, not a report about a client.

A caption could read:

Does your next home need an extra bedroom, or does it need somewhere you can close the door for work? Before we search, we turn each must-have into a question about how you use the space. That gives us a better way to compare layouts without assuming every solution needs another room. Write down one feature on your list and the job you need it to do. Bring both to our first conversation.

You can record that explanation at your desk. There is no need for a borrowed house photo or an invented success story. To develop more topics from your meetings, use the buyer consultation checklist.

Review the post before scheduling it

  • Check facts: open the original source for prices, dates, statistics, property features, and event details.
  • Check permissions: confirm you may use the images, listing information, and any quoted feedback.
  • Check wording: describe the property and services. Ask your broker to review questionable housing language and required advertising disclosures.
  • Check privacy: inspect screenshots and video backgrounds for client names, messages, signatures, or documents.
  • Check the destination: test the link and make sure its page delivers the promised information.

Use the site's Fair Housing wording guide and client-information privacy checklist when reviewing sensitive drafts.

On days 29 and 30, record saves, meaningful replies, website visits, and inquiries you can trace to the posts. Compare those results with the time you spent preparing them. A post that starts a useful buyer conversation may deserve another version even if its view count looks modest. Avoid claiming a sale came from a post unless you can support that connection.

Common questions

How often should a real estate agent post?

Start with the three-post weekly schedule above if it fits your workload. Reduce it to two if checking facts and replying to readers becomes difficult. Treat frequency as something to test against your audience and capacity.

Can I use ChatGPT to make a real estate content calendar?

You can use a chat tool to organize topics and draft captions from your notes. You still need to check its output, supply permitted visuals, and publish or schedule the posts through your chosen platform.

Can I reuse the same idea on Instagram and Facebook?

Yes. Keep the underlying facts consistent, then adapt the format and next step. A carousel can become a short explanation with an image. Check that the link or contact method works in the place you publish it.

What should I post if I am a new agent?

Explain the process you are trained and authorized to provide: preparing for a consultation, organizing showing feedback, or getting a home ready for photos. Do not imply you completed transactions or earned testimonials you do not have.
